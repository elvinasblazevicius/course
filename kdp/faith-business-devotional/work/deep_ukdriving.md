### Observations
- **Official vs. AA vs. indie [V]:** one DVSA/TSO official title clearly dominates (BSR 518). AA's 2022 all-in-one still sells (BSR 8,012) even though it is four years old. - **Ratings on the official book are split by edition [V]:** the 2025 edition shows only 207 ratings, while the 2019 edition shows 10,014. Each new edition starts its rating count from a low base.
- **Signs of review manipulation [V for the facts, A for the meaning]:** the Suburban Press car book (B0FDFTZT42, 4.9 / 133) shows Spanish-language reviews about *manga* from 2019–2024, so the ratings appear to be merged or borrowed from another product. The Hayes book has several 5★ reviews posted from Italy within a few days of each other in Feb 2026. The Harold and Richardson listings both show the same 1★ review text, which suggests (A) the same publishing operation is behind both, under different pen names.
- **Title conventions [V]:** almost every indie title uses "DVSA" ("1000 DVSA Practice Questions", "DVSA Revision Questions", "DVSA & DVLA Questions"), a year range, a question count (400–1,500), "Mock Exams" and "Pass First Time". Several put "DVSA-style" in the description while the title says "DVSA Questions".
- **Formats [V]:** indie titles run 106–266 pp and usually have answers directly under the questions. DVSA and AA run 460–504 pp.

### Complaint clusters
1. **"Not the official questions" / misleading "DVSA" claims** (#1, #3, #6, #9, #18). This is the main problem in the niche. Buyers who expect the real bank feel cheated.
2. **Answer-key position bias**: "nearly every answer is A / B / C" (#1, #6, #7). This happened in three separate indie books, and is a typical sign of unshuffled AI-generated multiple-choice questions [A].
3. **Wrong or inconsistent answers, typos, missing answers** (#1, #8, #11, #16).
4. **Answers printed directly under the questions**, so readers cannot self-test (#2, #16; AA and Hayes 4★ reviews too).
5. **Black-and-white or missing road signs and images** (#11, #16, #17; Hayes 4★ "no pictures"; even the DVSA book is criticised for having no road-sign section).
6. **Thin content or "rehashes the Highway Code"** (#7, #10).
7. **Compared unfavourably with the official app (around £5–6) or free online tests** (#6, #8, #9).
8. **Hazard perception cannot be practised from print** (#19, #20; DVSA 2016 5★ review).

### Praise clusters (from 4–5★ reviews) [V]
- Structure, studying chapter by chapter, and spotting weak areas: "I could go chapter by chapter and actually see my weak areas" (Langford).
- **Colour road signs**: "Very glad the road signs are in colour" (Langford).

## 3. Copyright and licensing

### DVSA revision question bank: licence terms [V, gov.uk "Make and sell driving theory test revision products", published 29 Jul 2025, updated 23 Feb 2026]
- "You must apply for a copyright licence from the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) before you make a product that includes: revision theory test questions; practice hazard perception clips."
- "It's a criminal offence to use DVSA theory test questions or hazard perception clips in a product without a licence. You can be prosecuted and fined."
- Licence types: Standard (English and Welsh, **3 years**), BSL, Translation. **No cost to apply.** A decision comes within 10 working days, followed by a video call about the product and business plan, then a signed "Crown copyright licence agreement".
- The licensee receives the revision questions and answers plus "knowledge and understanding text", which "must be included with every question in your product". DVSA describes them as "not the exact questions that are used in the real test, but they're very similar."
- **Royalties:** 13% of net sales each quarter if 50% or more of the product is DVSA material, or a pro-rata lower rate if less (e.g. 20% licensed content means 20% × 13%). VAT is added for UK businesses. Signed quarterly statements are required, nil statements included. Late payment interest is Bank Rate + 2.5%.
- Hazard perception practice clips (MPEG-4): **£550 to £1,200** depending on the number of clips.
- **Artwork approval:** DVSA approves the product artwork before sale. The product must "not look like an official DVSA product", "not display the DVSA logo", "not imply or suggest that it's recommended, endorsed or approved by DVSA", and must carry this statement: "The Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) has given permission for the reproduction of Crown copyright material. DVSA is not responsible for the accuracy of the reproduction."
- DVSA "constantly monitors the use of its material" and has a public form for reporting infringement.
- Contact: crowncopyright@dvsa.gov.uk.
- [A] It is unclear how "net sales" applies to a KDP paperback (list price less VAT, or the royalty Amazon pays the author). This would need to be confirmed with DVSA before costing a licensed edition.
- [V] Licensed products exist and say so openly: theorytest.org.uk "Crown Copyright material reproduced under licence from the DVSA"; an App Store app advertises "DVSA-licensed questions"; drivingtheorytest.io carries the required statement. A licensed print book appears to be an available, legitimate route [A: I did not verify that DVSA licenses KDP print books specifically, but the guidance explicitly covers "books"].

### Can an indie write original questions instead? [A, with [V] support]
- The licence requirement covers DVSA's questions and clips. Nothing in the guidance stops anyone writing **original** questions based on the Highway Code (OGL) and on the topics the test covers. Several indie books say they are "independently produced… No question is…" taken from the bank (the High Level Press HGV/PCV listings [V]).
- Risks: (a) paraphrasing DVSA bank questions too closely could still infringe [A]; (b) titles claiming "DVSA questions" for original content are what draw the "it's a lie" reviews [V], and may break KDP's metadata rules (below) [A].

### Highway Code and traffic signs: OGL [V]
- The gov.uk licensing page says: "You do not need to apply for a licence if you only want to use content from The Highway Code or traffic sign images. However, you must follow the rules in the Open Government Licence."
- OGL v3.0: you may "copy, publish, distribute and transmit… adapt… exploit the Information commercially". You must acknowledge the source ("Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0" unless a specific statement is given). Exemptions: logos and crests, third-party rights, trade marks.
- **Non-endorsement clause:** "This licence does not grant you any right to use the Information in a way that suggests any official status or that the Information Provider and/or Licensor endorse you."
- Traffic sign images (DfT, more than 600 images in JPG/EPS): "You may reproduce traffic-sign images free of charge and without having to seek permission, but you must reproduce them accurately and not in a misleading context… include a statement that these images are Crown copyright."
- So the Highway Code text and the sign images can be quoted or reproduced with attribution. Colour sign images are free, which removes the excuse for black-and-white signs.

### "DVSA" or "Official" in the title
- DVSA licence rules [V]: even licensees must not "look like an official DVSA product" or imply endorsement. OGL [V]: no use "that suggests any official status".
- KDP metadata guidelines [V] prohibit in titles: "Unauthorized reference to a trademarked term" and "References that suggest an individual or entity has contributed to or been involved with your book that has not".
- Conclusion [A, strongly supported]: **do not put "Official" in the title.** "DVSA Questions" or "DVSA Practice Questions" for unlicensed original content is misleading. A descriptive phrase such as "for the DVSA car theory test", plus a clear "independent, not affiliated with or endorsed by DVSA" statement (as the McBride, Ellison and High Level Press listings use [V]), is the defensible pattern. I did not verify whether "DVSA" itself is a registered trade mark [unverified]. DVSA does run a "report misuse of DVSA logos" form [V].
- ASA: a 2018 ASA item concerned services that "misleadingly implied an affiliation to the government or official body (such as the DVLA…)" [V, search snippet only; that was about copycat websites, not books].

### Hazard perception: a book cannot fully cover it [V]
- The real test has two parts, and you must pass both. Multiple choice: 50 questions in 57 minutes, pass mark 43; three of the questions are based on a short silent video. Hazard perception: **14 video clips**, scored 0–5 per developing hazard, pass mark 44 out of 75. Clicking "continuously or in a pattern" scores nothing (gov.uk).
- A print book can only teach the principles (what a developing hazard is, timing, cues) and point to video practice. Licensed clips cost £550–£1,200 [V]. The legitimacy of hosting them behind QR codes from a book would depend on the licence terms [A].
- Reviewers already discount books on this point (#19, #20, DVSA 2016 5★).

## 4. Currency (2025–2026 changes)

- **CPR/AED questions** [V, DVSA news 13 Aug 2025]: "From 2026, driving theory tests will include new CPR questions and, for the first time, questions about defibrillators." Car and motorcycle candidates should study these "from autumn 2025". There is "no additional cost, test time, or difficulty". DVSA's official car books have already been updated, and the DVSA 2025 edition's blurb lists "New questions and study content about first aid" [V]. The same news item states that **50 questions are drawn from a bank of over 700**, that around **2.4 million theory tests** are taken each year, and that the pass rate was **45.7% (Jul–Sep 2024)** [V].
- **Accuracy trap** [R, drivingtheorytest.io]: Highway Code Annex 7 still teaches DR-ABC, while the new CPR questions follow Resuscitation Council UK 2025 guidance (check, call 999, CPR; 100–120 compressions a minute; 5–6 cm depth). The DVSA example question confirms 5–6 cm [V].
- **Highway Code amendments** [V, gov.uk updates page]: 22 Oct 2025 (bus/cycle-only and tram-only signs no longer need an "only" plate); 10 Apr 2025 (Scotland: no parking at dropped kerbs or on pavements, rules 243–244); 17 Sep 2023 (Wales 20 mph default, rules 113/124); 2022 (hierarchy of road users H1–H3, mobile phone rule 149, self-driving vehicles). Amendments come irregularly (several small ones a year, and a big overhaul in Jan 2022). The online gov.uk version is always current; the printed TSO edition is dated 1 Apr 2022 [V].
- **Announced or proposed** [V]: DfT consultation (7 Jan – 11 May 2026) on a **3- or 6-month minimum learning period between passing the theory test and taking the practical test**; government response pending. The Road Safety Strategy also proposes a lower drink-drive limit in England and Wales (BBC, 7 Jan 2026). Car practical test booking rules changed on 31 Mar, 12 May and 9 Jun 2026; these do not apply to the theory test [R].
- **Question bank updates** [R]: the bank is refreshed continuously, with no single annual switchover (examroutes.co.uk, Mar 2026). Topics given more weight lately: EVs, ADAS (the DVSA 2025 blurb [V]), smart motorways, vulnerable road users [R].
- **Scope:** the guidance covers England, Scotland and Wales. Northern Ireland has its own DVA (a separate McBride NI title exists [V]).
- **How buyers judge "up to date"** [V from listings and reviews; A for weighting]: the year in the title (indies already sell "2027–2028" in Sep 2026); "latest Highway Code" claims; CPR/AED in the subtitle (Hartwell); reviews saying "Updated questions", "Current and informative", "up-to-date questions"; and the latest DVSA edition date. Reviewers punish "old outdated questions" (#18).

## 5. Feasibility for an AI-assisted writer with fact-checking

### What the best book would do (from the review clusters)
1. Original, well-written questions with **answer positions randomised and balanced** (check the A/B/C/D distribution), and **answers at the back or on the reverse side**, keyed by question number.
2. **Full-colour, accurate DfT sign images** (free under OGL) with a dedicated road-signs section. Even the official book is criticised for lacking one.
3. Short explanations for every answer that cite the Highway Code rule number, so readers can check them.
4. Full 50-question mock exams in the real topic mix, scored against 43/50, plus a study plan and a weak-area tracker.
5. An honest hazard perception chapter on principles and timing, which points to official or licensed video practice. It should not claim "hazard perception training".
6. A current-content box covering CPR/AED (RCUK 2025), the Scotland parking rules, Wales 20 mph, the 2022 hierarchy and EV/ADAS, with an "edition date" and a corrections page.
7. Clear positioning: "independent companion to the official DVSA materials", not "the DVSA questions".
8. Optional: a properly **DVSA-licensed** edition (13% royalty, artwork approval). That would answer the main complaint, at the cost of margin and an approval step [A].

### How buyers expecting official questions react to original ones [V]
- They react badly and say so explicitly: "These are NOT official questions used by DVSA", "this is in fact a lie", "skip this and get the official documents. Get the official app".
- The official book is praised precisely because "nearly all the questions that came up was in the book".
- Original questions are tolerated or praised when the book is honest about them and well made (Langford 5.0/5, Martin 4.6/39), but the 1★ reviews cluster wherever "DVSA questions" appears in the title [V pattern; A causation].

### Realistic error risk [A, grounded in observed failures]
- **High-stakes domain:** readers fail the test if the answers are wrong, and they check answers against the app or the Highway Code.
- **Observed failure modes in competitors:** wrong answers marked correct; the same question answered differently in two places; kph instead of mph; missing answers; answer-position bias; outdated questions; blurry or black-and-white images.
- **Specific traps:** DR-ABC vs. the new CPR model; stopping distances; speed limits by vehicle type and road; the Scotland and Wales variations; motorway signals and red X; tyre tread and legal documents; alcohol limits (which may change); NI differences; and US or EU terms creeping into AI-drafted text (kph, "right of way", "license").
- With a disciplined workflow (every answer traced to a Highway Code rule number, a second-pass independent check, scripted answer-position shuffling, and a human proofread of every image), errors can be brought to a low rate. But a few errors in 800–1,000 questions is still likely [A], and each one is visible in reviews.
- Questions also have to be refreshed yearly so the edition year can be updated.

## Scores (for the caller)
- **Demand: 8/10.** Official book BSR 518, Highway Code BSR 66, the best indie at BSR 1,873, about 2.4M tests a year [V].
- **Competition (10 = easy to win): 4/10.** The official book has the real bank. AA is cheap. About 12 or more indie 2026–27 titles, some with apparently inflated reviews. Indie quality is low, so a well-made book can stand out [V/A].
- **Legal/licensing safety: 6/10.** Safe with original questions, OGL Highway Code and signs, and no "DVSA questions" or "Official" claims. Risky if you copy the market's title conventions. A licensed route exists at 13% [V/A].
- **Feasibility/accuracy risk (10 = low risk): 5/10.** The content is well defined and open, but the domain is high-stakes, rules vary by nation, CPR content changed, and reviewers spot AI patterns [A].

**Verdict:** Viable only as an honestly positioned, high-quality companion book (original questions, colour signs, answers at the back, rule-referenced explanations) or as a DVSA-licensed edition. It cannot replace the official book.

**Biggest risk:** buyers want the real DVSA question bank. An unlicensed book can't give them that, and labelling original questions as "DVSA questions" (as most competitors do) brings "it's a lie" 1★ reviews and possible DVSA or KDP action. Every answer error also shows up publicly.
