## 0. Bottom line

| Dimension | Score /10 | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | **6** | Large, evergreen candidate pool (about 203k nursing-assistant/orderly openings a year, per BLS [V]). The best indie title sits at BSR #47,703 [V]. Free practice tests are everywhere. |
| Competition (10 = easy) | **3** | 20+ near-identical indie titles from 2025–26, many running ads, plus Mometrix and Test Prep Books annual editions and review-rich incumbents (321, 196 and 389 ratings). |
| Legal / trademark safety | **7** | NNAAP®, Prometric and Credentia are registered marks [V/partly]. Descriptive, nominative use plus a disclaimer is standard practice (Mometrix does it [V]). Avoid "Official", logos and copying handbook text verbatim. |
| Feasibility / accuracy risk (10 = low risk) | **5** | Official sources are free and very detailed (full NNAAP skill steps, Prometric checklists) [V], so fact-checking is practical. But skills differ by vendor and state, and a wrong answer or skill step is what earns 1★ reviews [V]. |

**Verdict:** Only viable as a sharply differentiated, vendor-specific book: for example, NNAAP (Credentia) 2024 outline plus the current 23-skill list, or Prometric's 22 skills for TX/FL/NY, with every step checked against the handbook. A generic "CNA study guide" would be a me-too entry in a saturated, ad-driven results page.

**Biggest risk:** Saturation. More than 20 look-alike 2025–26 indie guides plus brand annuals mean a new generic title will struggle to rank without ad spend. If it does rank, one wrong skill step or answer key brings the "inaccurate, do not buy" 1★ reviews that sink exam-prep books.

### Complaint themes (from the reviews above)

1. **Wrong or contradictory answers and rationales.** This is the most damaging theme (#1, Exam Cram temp range).
2. **Misleading quantity claims:** "500+ questions" that are really repeats (#4); "15 tests" with only 2 in print (TPB 4★); blank "flashcards" (#5); a book titled "Official" that isn't.
3. **Mismatch with the reader's state or vendor skills, or with class content** (#2, Simply Skills, TPB Vine review).
4. **Too dense or wordy, or too much filler** (#7, #8); "basic" (4★).
5. **No pictures or illustrations; poor typography or print** (Langford, Blake, Official guide).
6. **Price and value** for thin books (#3, Prometric guide 4★).
7. **Online bonus friction:** QR codes, account creation (TPB Vine review).

### Praise themes (top 5★ reviews, [V])

- Step-by-step skills that a study partner can drill ("She would tell me the required procedure, and I would walk through each step" — Official guide).
- Rationales for every answer ("they explained the reasoning behind the answer chosen" — Emory).
- A diagnostic pretest; practice tests that feel like the real format (Missouri reviewer on Mometrix PQ).
- Plain language that is easier than the textbook.
- Bilingual formats (Haitian Creole, Spanish).
- Portable size.
- Real "I passed" outcomes.

## 3. Trademarks, exam bodies, safe titling, and state fragmentation

### Who tests where (verified pages only; not a complete 50-state map)

- **NNAAP®, Credentia.**
  - Credentia's handbook knowledge base says: "The NNAAP was originally developed by the National Council of State Boards of Nursing Inc. (NCSBN)… Credentia is the authorized administrator" (PA) [V].
  - The 2024 outline says: "The NNAAP exam is now developed and managed by Credentia Nurse Aide LLC" [V].
  - Nevada Board CNA Advisory Committee minutes (3 Apr 2025) record a Credentia representative "Confirming that Credentia does own the NNAAP and MACE Examinations" [V].
  - A Maryland BON deck (Nov 2025) confirms Maryland on Credentia [V].
  - Credentia KB sites exist for PA, AL, WA, NC, TN, CO, RI, MD, Guam, DoDEA and CA-South, among others [V search results].
  - A 2021 NC DHHS presentation says Credentia acquired the program from Pearson VUE in 2021 [V search result]. **Pearson VUE no longer appears to run NNAAP** [A, inferred from the above].
- **Prometric** runs its own (non-NNAAP) nurse aide exams:
  - Florida: "The Florida Department of Health (DOH) has contracted with Prometric" [V]
  - New York: NYSDOH contract; paper certificates end 1 Aug 2026 [V]
  - Texas: new prices effective 1 Sep 2026 [V]
  - Idaho and Oklahoma pages also exist [V search]
  - Third-party lists add AL, CT, DE, HI, LA [A].
- **Headmaster / D&S Diversified Technologies (TMU©):**
  - California: CDPH-approved; handbook updates and fee increase effective 3-1-2026 [V]
  - Also MT, OR, ND, SD, OH, MA, WY per its pages [V search]
- **Other states** run their own programs or other vendors [A].

### Trademark status

- **NNAAP:** USPTO serial 77891928, reg. 3821655, owned by NCSBN, "Registered and Renewed" [V, Justia]. The goods covered include instruction booklets and test sheets. Credentia's own pages use "NNAAP®". **Ownership may have been assigned to Credentia; this was not verified at USPTO** [A].
- **CREDENTIA:** filed 10 Jul 2023 by Credentia Nurse Aide, LLC (serials 98078330, 98078323), plus "CREDENTIA NURSE AIDE CREDENTIALING SERVICES" [V Justia]. Registration status was not checked [A].
- **PROMETRIC:** reg. 4198709 (serial 77435464), owned by Prometric LLC. Justia shows Sec. 8 & 15 accepted, while another aggregator shows it cancelled, so the status is unclear [V conflicting]. Treat it as a trademark either way.
- **Headmaster / TMU©:** the TMU© notice is on its site [V]. No relevant "Headmaster" registration was found for testing [V search; inconclusive].
- **"CNA" / "Certified Nursing Assistant":** a generic job title; it does not trigger trademark issues [A, standard].

### Safe title and listing language [A, standard nominative fair use and KDP practice]

- **OK:** "CNA Exam Prep 2027: Study Guide for the Nurse Aide Written and Skills Exam". "Aligned to the 2024 NNAAP® Written Exam Content Outline" in the subtitle or description. "Covers Prometric-tested skills (TX, FL, NY)" in the description.
- **Add a disclaimer** like Mometrix's [V]: "not affiliated with or endorsed by any official testing organization. All organizational and test names are trademarks of their respective owners."
- **Avoid:**
  - "Official" (the Deborah Clark book does this; it is misleading)
  - Vendor names as the leading brand word (e.g., "PROMETRIC CNA TEST 2026"; indies do this, but it invites a rights-owner complaint)
  - Vendor logos or colors
  - "Real exam questions"
  - Pass guarantees
- **Copyright:** the Prometric checklist PDF says "Copyright © 2014 Prometric Inc. … All Rights Reserved" [V]. Credentia's handbook is © Credentia [V, deck footer]. Teach the steps in your own words and structure. Do not reproduce checklists verbatim. Cite and point readers to the free official handbook.

### How much the exam differs (fragmentation)

- **Written exam:**
  - NNAAP: 70 MCQ (60 scored + 10 pretest), 2 hours. Oral version: 60 MCQ + 10 word-recognition items [V].
  - Content weights: ADLs 22%, Basic Nursing Skills 35%, Self-care 7%, Emotional/Mental 8%, Spiritual/Cultural 2%, Communication 7%, Client Rights 8%, Legal/Ethical 5%, Health Care Team 6% [V].
  - Prometric and Headmaster use their own item banks and outlines [V that they are separate programs; outline details not fetched].
  - Knowledge content (ADLs, infection control, resident rights) is federally anchored (OBRA '87) and largely portable across states [V for OBRA framing; portability is A].
- **Skills exam (this is where fragmentation bites):**
  - **NNAAP (Credentia):** 23 listed skills. Hand hygiene is always tested, plus 4 random skills, one of which is a measurement skill. 30 minutes. All 5 must be passed. Critical-element steps are marked in bold. Pennsylvania does **not** test "bedpan" or "electronic BP"; electronic BP (#12) is "state specific" and not interchangeable with manual BP (#23). Tolerances: pulse ±4 bpm, respirations ±2, urine output ±25 mL, weight ±2 lb, BP ±8 mmHg [V, PA KB].
  - **Prometric:** "22 skills". Handwashing **and Indirect Care** are always scored, plus 3 other skills. The skill set differs: e.g., "Change bed linen while the resident remains in bed", "hand and nail care", "partial bed bath and back rub". In Florida, two nurses observe [V, FL checklist].
  - **Headmaster:** its own state skill lists. A Massachusetts indie title cites "All 19 State-Tested Skills" [V title; count not verified at the source].
  - Many indie books advertise "All 22 skills". That matches Prometric, but **not** the current NNAAP list of 23. These books are already partly wrong for some readers [V on both lists; the date electronic BP was added was not verified].
- **Demand effect.** Fragmentation splits the skills half of the market by vendor, and to some extent by state. A single national book must either cover three vendor variants (longer and more confusing; "check your state" complaints are common) or pick one. Florida, Texas and California each have dedicated indie titles that already rank; the Florida title is at #75k with 9 ratings [V].
  - [A] A vendor-specific book (NNAAP/Credentia: about 20+ jurisdictions; Prometric: TX, FL, NY plus others) has a large enough base.
  - [A] Single-state books trade reach for conversion.

## 4. Currency: recent changes and how fast books date

- **NNAAP 2024 outline** came from the 2019–2020 job analysis. Credentia pages give the effective date as April 2024 (PA) or October 2024 (NC/TN); the sources disagree [V]. The written format stays at 70 items [V].
- **Credentia review cadence:** "The CNA written and clinical exams will be reviewed every 2 to 3 years for possible updates, and the last update was in 2024" (Nevada minutes, Apr 2025) [V]. So a next revision is plausible in **2026–2027** [A].
- **Operational changes in 2025–2026** [V]:
  - Maryland scheduling lead time cut from 10 days to 2 days (Jul 2025)
  - Texas Prometric price change (1 Sep 2026)
  - California Headmaster handbook update and fee change (3-1-2026)
  - New York ends paper certificates (1 Aug 2026)
  - A third-party site claims Maryland renamed GNA to CNA-I and CNA to CNA-II as of 1 Apr 2026 [unverified].
- **No 2025–26 change to the NNAAP skills list was found** [V absence in searches]. The current Credentia list shows 23 skills including the state-specific electronic BP skill [V].
- **How fast books date:**
  - Mometrix: 5th ed 2023-24, 6th ed 2024-25, 7th ed "2026-2027" (Oct 2025) [V]
  - Test Prep Books: 8th ed Sep 2024, 9th ed Mar 2026 [V]
  - So the brands refresh roughly every 12–18 months, and buyers reward year-stamped titles.
  - Content truly changes only every 2–4 years. Fees and vendor logistics change yearly, so keep those out of the print text.
  - Plan a light annual refresh with a new year stamp, and a real revision when Credentia or Prometric publish a new outline or skill list [A].

## 5. Safety, accuracy and feasibility

- **Health-education limits [A, standard practice].** This is exam-prep and education content for an entry-level, scope-limited role, not medical advice to patients.
  - Include a disclaimer: "for exam preparation; follow your training program, facility policy and the nurse's instructions; not a substitute for state-approved training."
  - Credentia's own handbook says duties belonging to LPN/RN scope "are deemed inappropriate for a nurse aide to perform" [V]. The book must not drift into nursing-scope tasks (medication administration, assessment, sterile procedures) except to teach "report to the nurse".
  - KDP requires disclosure of AI-generated content at upload [A, known KDP policy; not re-fetched today].
- **Are the official sources free?** Yes [V]:
  - Credentia publishes full state handbooks online (PA KB: all 23 skills, step by step, with critical-element notes, tolerances and test rules).
  - Prometric posts the Candidate Information Bulletin, the "exact checklist that will be used by your evaluator", timing summaries, indirect-care behaviours and measurement forms.
  - Headmaster posts candidate handbooks and skill-test instructions per state.
  - Credentia/Prometric handbooks include sample written questions [V MD deck: "Sample examination questions are available … in the Maryland candidate handbook"].
  - Caveat: Prometric warns its checkpoints "are not written as procedures… procedures… are learned from your instructor, nurse aide textbooks" [V]. A book that bridges checkpoints into a teachable procedure fills a real gap.
- **Realistic error risk for an AI-assisted writer with fact-checking [A]:**
  - *Skills steps:* risk is **low to moderate** if every step is checked line by line against the chosen vendor's current list. Steps are concrete (e.g., 20-second lather, fingertips down, count a full minute, round urine up to the nearest 25 mL). The typical failures are:
    - mixing vendors (NNAAP vs Prometric steps and order dependencies; "indirect care" is a Prometric concept)
    - missing critical elements
    - wrong tolerances
    - outdated counts ("22 skills")
  - *Written practice questions:* risk is **moderate**. Generated MCQs often produce ambiguous stems, two defensible answers, or rationales that contradict the key. That is exactly the 1★ complaint above. Knowledge facts (normal vital-sign ranges, positioning angles such as 75–90° upright for feeding, C. diff precautions requiring soap-and-water handwashing, resident rights under OBRA) need a cited source per item: the handbook, CDC, or a standard text such as Hartman/Mosby.
  - [A] Estimated workload: 300–500 questions with rationales need a two-pass human review. Budget about 2–3 minutes per item, which is 15–25 hours.
- **What makes the best book (from reviews plus the official structure):**
  1. **Vendor-specific skills section.** One spread per skill, in your own words: supplies, numbered steps, flagged "critical" steps, "order-dependent" cues ("before standing…"), common fail points, a partner check-off box, and simple line illustrations. Reviewers ask for pictures.
  2. **Measurement drills:** practice reading BP gauge faces, graduate cylinders and scales, with stated tolerances.
  3. **Written prep mapped to the official outline percentages,** with a diagnostic pretest. Reviewers praise pretests.
  4. **All practice tests printed in the book,** with a full rationale per option, unique non-repeating questions, and an honest count on the cover. Online extras are optional; don't make them the headline.
  5. **Test-day rules:** volunteer "client" role, corrections procedure, timing, what you can't bring.
  6. **Plain-language and ESL-friendly writing,** a glossary, and optionally a bilingual edition (Spanish or Haitian Creole niches are proven [V]).
  7. **A "check your state" pointer page** with links to each vendor's free handbook. Don't hard-code fees.
