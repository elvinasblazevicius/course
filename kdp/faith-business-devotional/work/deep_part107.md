# Deep dive: FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot study guide and practice tests (KDP, Amazon.com)

Research date: 2026-10-15. Tools: TinyFish fetch/search (free) and WebSearch. No paid automation was used.

**Legend.** **[V]** means verified this session from a fetched page; the source is named. **[A]** means assumed, inferred or my own calculation. **[U]** means looked for but not confirmed.

Amazon.com product pages often returned a bot page. A few loaded through slug URLs (`/Title-Slug/dp/ASIN`), and BSRs come from those. Amazon.com `product-reviews` pages returned 404 or a bot page. On .com.au and .in they required a login. Review texts therefore come from **amazon.co.uk and amazon.com.au product pages**. Those pages show **US reviews** under "Top reviews from other countries", plus Amazon's AI "Customers say" excerpts. Ratings counts on .co.uk/.com.au are "global ratings" and differ slightly from .com.

### 1c. Observations

- **Quality leaders.** Bolton is the full-colour, 8.5x11 indie leader. ASA is the legacy brand: it bundles Prepware and the FAA figures, and its 2027-28 listing now opens with *"This is not AI-generated content… written and verified by subject matter experts"* [V .ca]. The incumbent is openly using "human-written" as a selling point against AI-made indie books.
- **The 2025-2026 influx.** Since late 2025 there has been a wave of indie books with big question-count claims: Atlas, Ashwick, ACA, Sky Academic, Lemay, Brennan, Calhoon. Their BSRs run from 71k to 146k [V].
- **Inconsistent claims.** The ACA title says "1,200 Practice Questions". Its description says "700+ Original Questions". Both are [V] on the same page.
- **Top sellers by BSR are cheap FAA reprints.** The AKTS reprint at #7,405 outsells every study guide. Buyers clearly want the exact test-centre booklet in colour.
- **Kindle pricing.** Bolton and Ashwick both sell Kindle at $9.99 [V]. Brennan is in Kindle Unlimited [V].

### Complaint groups (ranked by frequency in what I saw)

1. **Missing or bad charts.** No sectional images, B&W or low-resolution charts, blurry figures, tiny print (#2, 3, 14, 20, 21, 23, 27, 28, 30). This is the biggest theme. Chart questions are heavier than buyers expect (#33).
2. **Errors and poor editing.** Wrong acronyms (IMSAFE), wrong conversions (SM to NM), typos, "false information", wrong answers (#1, 8, 12, 13, 17, 22).
3. **Outdated material.** Old category percentages, rule changes, "missing information for 2026" (#7, 8, 29).
4. **Thin content or inflated question counts.** "Pamphlet", "brief overview", "where is 15 full tests", repetitive questions, only "10-15%" of questions match the real test (#1, 4, 9, 15, 16, 19, 21).
5. **AI-generated suspicion** (#1). The incumbent ASA now markets "not AI-generated" in its listing.
6. **Software / online quality** (#6, 7).
7. **Reprint resentment.** "copied pages… slop" (#25), and "the last half of the book is the AKTS" (#7).

### Praise themes [V, Bolton and ASA highlight panels and reviews]

## 3. Copyright and trademark

### Public domain (FAA works)

- **17 U.S.C. §105** [V, law.cornell.edu]: copyright protection "is not available for any work of the United States Government." The House report text says the intent is "to place all works of the United States Government, published or unpublished, in the public domain." A "work of the US Government" is one "prepared by an officer or employee… as part of that person's official duties."
- This therefore covers the following, all prepared by FAA staff:
  - 14 CFR Part 107 (regulation text, eCFR)
  - FAA-G-8082-22 Remote Pilot Study Guide
  - FAA-S-ACS-10B Airman Certification Standards
  - FAA-CT-8080-2H testing-supplement figures
  - FAA sectional charts (produced by FAA Aeronautical Information Services)
  - AC 107-2A
  - the FAA's own UAG sample questions
- These can be reproduced and sold [V for the statute; applying it to each document is **[A]**]. The many Amazon reprints of FAA-CT-8080-2H and FAA-G-8082-22 sold for years are practical evidence [V].
- **The FAA's own handbook pages state no copyright reservation.** A text search of FAA-G-8082-22 found no "copyright" or "courtesy" credits [V]. I could not fetch the CT-8080-2H PDF to check for figure credits [U].

### Limits and caveats

- **§105 covers only works by federal employees.** Material made by a contractor or a third party inside an FAA document can still be copyrighted (House report text [V]). Some FAA handbooks (for example, PHAK) credit manufacturer photos [A, not re-checked]. Check every figure you reuse for a credit line.
- **The §105 bar does not apply abroad.** The House report says the prohibition "is not intended to have any effect on protection of these works abroad" [V]. This is low risk for amazon.com, but in theory it matters for UK/EU marketplaces [A].
- **Third-party chart products are copyrighted.** Never copy images from Jeppesen, ForeFlight, SkyVector screenshots, Drone Pilot Ground School, ASA or Bolton figures, or competitors' question wording. Use only the FAA GeoTIFF/PDF raster charts, which the FAA publishes for free at faa.gov VFR Raster Charts [V].
- **KDP public-domain policy** [V, KDP help G200743940]. If a free version exists, KDP accepts only "differentiated" versions: translated, annotated, or illustrated with 10 or more original illustrations. "Freely available Internet content" and "formatting improvements" do not count. Public-domain content "is not eligible for all eBook royalty options or KDP Select."
  - *Implication [A]:* a book that is mostly original explanations and questions, with FAA figures inside it, should be published as your own copyrighted work. Do not pad it with reprinted regulation text or whole supplement pages, or KDP may classify it as public domain. That would cost KDP Select / Kindle Unlimited and the 70% eBook royalty.

### Trademark and implied endorsement

- **FAA Order 1700.6D** (FAA Branding Policy, effective 2024-02-27) [V via search summary of faa.gov] bars using the FAA logo or logomark in any way that suggests FAA, DOT or US government endorsement of a product. **The DOT seal order 1000.14A** has a similar bar [V, search summary]. So: **no FAA logo, DOT seal or look-alike wings on the cover.**
- **Using the words "FAA" and "Part 107" descriptively in a title** is standard and widely tolerated. It is used by Bolton, ASA ("pass the FAA Knowledge Test"), the QuickStudy laminate, Drone Boss and about ten others [V listings]. I found no FAA trademark registration or enforcement against descriptive use [U].
- **Avoid "Official", "FAA-approved" or "FAA-endorsed", and do not list "Federal Aviation Administration" as author.** Add a disclaimer: "not affiliated with or endorsed by the FAA". Whether 18 U.S.C. §709 (misuse of federal agency names) covers the FAA was not verified [U]. The safe practice holds either way.
- **"ASA", "Prepware", "Bolton Prep" and "Drone Pilot Ground School" are private brands.** Do not use them in keywords or titles [A].

**Legal verdict:** low risk if the book is original and uses only FAA-sourced figures.

### How fast books date

- **Test changes.** There have been three material test-format changes in about 5.5 years: April 2021, September 2025 and October 2026. A fourth is likely with Part 108 in 2026-27 [A].
- **ASA's response.** ASA re-issues every two years (2025-26, then 2027-28) and offers free online updates.
- **Competitors already behind.** Within months of the September 2025 blueprint change, reviewers flagged ASA's Prepware as using "old percentages" (#7). The FAA's own study guide is ten years stale.
- **Plan accordingly.** Expect a 12-24-month shelf life per edition, a mid-cycle errata or update page, and a forced revision when Part 108 is final [A].
- **Opportunity.** Most current books were written before 2025-09-29 or before the 2026-10-26 change. A book built around "48% regulations" and "read unseen, current sectionals" is clearly differentiated right now [A].

## 5. Feasibility for an AI-assisted writer with fact-checking

### What the best book would contain [A, built from the review evidence]

- **Size and trim.** 8.5x11 large trim, 200-260 pages. That matches Bolton (213), ASA (264) and ACA (229).
- **Colour.** Full colour, or at least standard colour, for every chart and figure. B&W or low-resolution charts are the top complaint, and colour charts are the top praise.
- **Charts.** Use **current FAA sectional excerpts with their edition date printed**, mirroring the FAA's new caption style ("Sectional: Las Vegas, dated…"). Add a chart-reading *procedure* chapter with 60-100 chart questions on charts that are *not* in CT-8080-2H. This answers the 2026-10-26 change.
- **Question volume.**
  - 4-5 full 60-question exams weighted to the **current blueprint**, about 29/15/12/3/1 per exam
  - plus 150-250 chapter drills
  - **about 400-550 high-quality questions in total**
  - Every answer explains why each wrong option is wrong and cites the governing source (§107.x, AC 107-2A, AIM, Chart User's Guide).
  - Inflated counts like "3,000" or "3,500" draw ridicule (#1, #4).
- **Updates.** An "As of" date page and a public errata/updates URL. A short section on Remote ID, Operations Over People categories, night operations, ALC-677 recurrent training, and a "Part 108 watch" box.
- **Kindle edition.** Hyperlinked answer and back links, which Bolton is praised for.

### KDP print cost (8.5x11 large trim, amazon.com) [V rates, KDP help G201834340; maths A]

| Interior | Formula | 250 pp cost | Royalty at $29.99 (60%) | Royalty at $34.99 | Royalty at $39.99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Black ink | $1.00 + $0.017/pp | **$5.25** | $12.74 | $15.74 | $18.74 |
| Standard colour (72-600 pp) | $1.00 + $0.0402/pp | **$11.05** | $6.94 | $9.94 | $12.94 |
| Premium colour | $1.00 + $0.08/pp | **$21.00** | not allowed (minimum list $35.00) | not allowed (just under the $35.00 minimum) | $2.99 |

- **Reading the table.** Premium colour across the whole book is uneconomic. Bolton-style full colour at 213 pages would cost about $18.04 to print, giving about $4.75 royalty at its $37.99 list price, if it is printed on KDP premium (not verified; Bolton uses its own ISBN imprint) [A].
- **Recommended format [A].** Standard colour, 8.5x11, 220-250 pages, list price **$29.99-$34.99**, giving about $7-10 per paperback. Kindle at $9.99-14.99 with colour figures. Optional hardcover.
- **Alternative.** A B&W interior with a free colour-chart PDF is cheaper, but it invites the "B&W charts unreadable" complaint.

### Error risk [A, grounded in the evidence above]

- **The sources are bounded and free.** Part 107, ACS-10B, AC 107-2A, CT-8080-2H, FAA samples, FAA rasters. That makes fact-checking tractable.
- **Mistakes are severe and public.** Typical failures look like the Atlas IMSAFE error and the Lehman SM/NM error. Buyers include instructors (#1).
- **AI-generated chart questions are the highest-risk item.** Right answers depend on exact symbol reading: airspace floors, MEF, CTAF, Class E surface areas. **Every chart question must be hand-checked against the actual chart edition.**
- **Mis-attribution.** A practitioner audit found that over half of rule attributions in a 460-question bank were wrong, even though the answers held (Drone Girl article [V]). Cited sources are a further error surface.
- **Realistic target.** Without human subject-matter review, I would expect visible errors in a 500-question bank [A]. With two-pass verification (source citation for every item plus an independent Part 107-certified reviewer working through all exams), the residual risk is manageable.
- **Budget.** Allow 40-80 hours of review [A].

### Pricing and position [A]

- **Pricing.** Paperback $29.99-$34.99. That is above Atlas and ACA ($20-23), around ASA ($26-30) and Ashwick ($30-35), and below Bolton's list ($37.99), though Bolton sells at $22.99.
- **Position.** "Current for the Oct-2026 test: 48% regulations + unseen-chart reading, every answer sourced, human-verified."

|---|---|---|
| Demand | **7/10** | 73,914 UAG tests in 2025 [V]. The supplement reprint is at BSR 7.4k and Bolton at 20k [V]. A steady professional need, mostly paperback. |

**Verdict:** worth doing only as a standard-colour 8.5x11 book, human-verified question by question, built around the October 2026 test format. A generic AI-made question bank would lose in this field.

**Biggest risk:** accuracy and currency combined. One wrong chart answer or an outdated rule draws "AI money grab" reviews in a crowded field, and the Part 108 final rule (at OIRA, possibly due by end of 2026) could date the first edition within months.
