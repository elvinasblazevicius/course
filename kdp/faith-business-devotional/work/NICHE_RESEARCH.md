## 3. Review matrix (54 critical reviews, plus Amazon's "customers say" summaries)

Shipping and condition complaints (about 30 of the 54: dirty, mould, bent, stickers) are the retailer's, not the book's, and are excluded below.

**AVOID (what readers hated) → our decision**
1. Scripture used out of context, or "not rooted in Scripture": "throws Bible verses into it to make it seem Godly", "verses taken out of context", "not biblically sound" (You Got This ×2, Believing Bigger ×3, Purpose and Power). → Every devotion reads its verse in context (who said it, to whom, why) before applying it. Theology checked against the passage; no stretched readings.
2. Self-exaltation in place of God ("puts us in the place of God", "declare what we want until we see it happen"). → No name-it-and-claim-it, no prosperity promises, no guaranteed outcomes (also the skill's rule).
3. Factual Bible errors (Esther on Day 76). → Every Bible fact checked against the KJV/WEB text.
4. "Nothing stood out", "meh", "writing had nothing to do with the scripture". → Each day tied to one real business moment and its verse.
5. Repetitive ("the same message over and over"). → 90 distinct business situations; overlap scan at every checkpoint.
6. No application, vague prompts ("no tools… or how we could change"). → One concrete, doable action each day, plus one specific prompt.
7. Prompts "written from doubt or fear". → Prompts framed with agency.
8. "Not about business… very short… blank pages" (the closest competitor, Boss Lady Believer). → Business-specific every day; real substance per entry; no padding pages.
9. "Makes me believe this is an AI rendered product." → The skill's human-voice rules and blind tests; this niche checks for it.
10. Belittling or narrowing women's roles (Purpose and Power; "belittled as a mom and wife"). → Respect every reader: single or married, mother or not, side business or full-time.
11. "Smaller than I thought" / "for the price I expected full size". → State trim and page count clearly; full-size paperback.

**WANTED (what 4–5 star readers praise; table stakes)**
- Short, quick daily read that "keeps me grounded"; a "perfect morning read".
- Relatable: "connects the Word to everyday struggles".
- Practical, with application questions.
- Gift-worthy (many buyers gift it; hardcover matters).
- Devotion plus journal balance (write-in space).
- Real-life stories (Prayer Powered Entrepreneur). Our honest equivalent: second-person scenes and plainly hypothetical pictures, never invented testimonies.

## 4. Main competitor and win plan (summary; full table goes in COMPETITOR_PLAN.md at N1 sign-off)

- **Main competitor (the category's best seller):** *You Got This* (DaySpring, #2,228). Broad "hardworking women", 90 days, scripture plus message plus questions, $8.85, small gift trim.
- **Closest-positioned competitors:** *Faith & Business: An Entrepreneur's Weekly Devotional & Journal* (#82k, $22, 52 weekly entries, unisex) and *The Prayer Powered Entrepreneur* (#206k, 31 days).

**Three winning dimensions (draft positioning):**
1. **Specific:** written only for the woman who runs her own business. Each day is a real owner moment: pricing, a client who won't pay, a slow month, comparison online, a hard no, hiring the first helper, rest versus hustle, quitting a product. The main competitor is general, and the closest competitor is weekly and unisex.
2. **Sound:** Scripture read in context, with no prosperity promises. This answers the most repeated content complaint in the category.
3. **Useful:** one small, concrete action every day, so faith meets Monday morning. This answers "no application" and "nothing to do with business".

**Standout mechanism (draft):** "Word, Work, Step". Each day pairs one verse, read in its setting, with one business moment and one 10-minute step. The step is something the reader actually does in her business that day (send the invoice, write the price down, close the laptop at six).

## 5. Rules check (skill section 3)
- **Category:** open (Elvin 2026-10-09: "boss babe", women's entrepreneurship; 2026-10-11: prayers and devotionals). Scripture from KJV/WEB only, tradition named (Christian, non-denominational Protestant-friendly), no promised outcomes, no income claims, no MLM, no personal financial, legal or tax advice.
- **Emotion:** strong. The reader is tired, alone with decisions, and anxious about money. The book's arc moves from strain to steadiness and quiet confidence.
- **Series potential:** high. "90 Devotions for…" as a family: women in business, women starting over after 50, nurses, teachers, women in ministry, single moms. Same brand and cover system.
- **Size:** 90 entries of about 250–300 words, plus intro and back matter, is about 25–30k words. That is Elvin's ideal Medium length and the shape the comps prove. Not sized to budget (Elvin, 2026-10-15).
- **Formats:** Kindle, paperback, hardcover. A gift-heavy category, so hardcover matters.
- **Humor:** gentle and sparing; none in the prayer lines (principle 17).
- **Would Elvin buy it himself?** He is not the reader. The book rests on the market evidence, not on personal experience. Flag for the panel.

**Gate: NOT PASSED** (floor is 9 on every angle).

**Root causes (grouped across all five):**
1. **Exact-niche demand is unproven.** Ranks are a one-day snapshot, there is no keyword volume, and the parent demand may not reach a narrow title. Fix: track ranks for 2–3 weeks on *Faith & Business*, *The Gift of Great Work* and *Built to Shine*; get Amazon keyword volume; check the missed competitors (*90 Days of Biblical Affirmations for Christian Women in Business and Ministry* Vol. II, *Rise To Your Purpose*, *Fearless Faith + Hustle*, *By The Book*).
2. **Positioning is too narrow.** Re-angle the reader to "a woman with her own business, any size", naming the side business and the kitchen-table shop, but not corporate leaders. Title on the owner's lived moment.
3. **Trust gap for a pen name in a niche that buys the author** (about 63% of recent religion titles are suspected AI). Fix: one translation (WEB, "LORD" edition, quoted verbatim, with a copyright-page statement); an honest "how this book was made and what it believes" page (Apostles' Creed baseline); a paid human theology reviewer credited truthfully (needs Elvin); no implied testimony.
4. **To-do-list risk.** Every 7th day is a Sabbath day with no task; some steps are spiritual; steps are framed "with God".
5. **Over-50 share is 31%, below 35%**, and the series is incoherent. Re-angle the series to stay with women in business: Book 2 starting after 50, Book 3 the first year, Book 4 a prayer book. Screen more over-50 angles and "100 Steps".
6. **The faith pivot needs Elvin's explicit OK.**

**Settled facts for the next round:** KJV is out (UK Crown patent, Cambridge's 500-verse non-commercial limit). "Boss Babe", "She Means Business", "Proverbs 31" (in titles and series), "Kingdom CEO" and "Faith Driven Entrepreneur" are out. *Pray First, Then Price It* is dropped (reads as prosperity and pricing advice).
