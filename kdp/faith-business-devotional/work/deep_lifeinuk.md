| Dimension | Score /10 | One-liner |

**Verdict:** Worth doing only as a short-life, low-cost, very high-quality "practice questions + explanations" book, with a planned rewrite the moment the new handbook lands. The bigger prize is being first to market on the **new** handbook (est. late 2027–2028 [A]).
**Biggest risk:** Obsolescence. The Home Office is procuring a new "Secure Digital Test and Digital Online Learning Materials" service with a contract start of **24 Nov 2027** [S enable.consulting citing Find-a-Tender]. TSO's materials contract has been extended to **30 Mar 2028** [V ojeu.com notice FTS/095324-2026]. A 2026 launch has perhaps 12–18 months of shelf life before the content may change.

## 3. Copyright, licensing, title and trademark

**Status of the handbook**
- *Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents* (3rd ed., ISBN 978-0-11-341340-9) is authored by the Home Office and published by TSO [V Amazon/TSO shop]. As a Home Office work it is **Crown copyright** [V: third-party reproductions state "crown copyright… reproduced from the government publication Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents (ISBN 978-0-11-341340-9)… with the permission of The National Archives under the Open Government License" (law.stackexchange snippet; the page itself was bot-blocked); passlifeinuk.co.uk: "Crown Copyright material reproduced under the Open Government Licence v3.0"].
- **OGL: unconfirmed from the primary source.** I could not see the book's own copyright page. Evidence for OGL: Public.Resource.Org (2013) built an open version, saying it "has the kind of open license one has come to expect" and thanking the Archivist of the UK [V boingboing.net, 4 Jun 2013]. Red Squirrel and Hugh Lewis/Dux sell books that reproduce the full testable text commercially (Red Squirrel since at least 2019 [V]). Evidence against unrestricted reuse: law.resource.org's open version now reads "Due to a takedown notice from TSO, after 11 years on the net, the study guide has been removed from public view" [V]. The Home Office–TSO contract also has "intellectual property arrangements" [V ojeu notice]. [A] TSO likely controls the typesetting, photos and third-party images, and may assert rights over the *published edition*, even if the text is OGL.
- The OGL v3 conditions themselves are [V nationalarchives.gov.uk]. It allows copying, adapting and commercial use with attribution ("Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0"). It excludes logos, crests, Royal Arms and third-party rights. A **non-endorsement clause** forbids use "in a way that suggests any official status or that the Information Provider… endorse you".

**Can a third party summarise it and write original practice questions?** Yes, with low risk [A, based on general UK copyright principles: facts and ideas are not protected, only expression].
- Original summaries in your own words, original questions, explanations, mnemonics and tables are your own copyright.
- Avoid copying the handbook's sentences at length, and never copy TSO's official Practice Q&A questions (which are not shown to be OGL) or the TSO layout and photos.
- If you quote handbook text, keep it short and add the OGL attribution.
- Never claim to include "real test questions". PSI's rules list "Copying any part of the test… and removing it from the test centre" as cheating [V lituktestbooking.co.uk FAQ]. Hugh Lewis's listing claims "The questions come from people who have passed the test; they are real questions" [V], which is a risky claim to imitate.

**"Life in the UK" in the title**
- No trademark registration for "Life in the UK" was found in a web search. The UKIPO database could not be queried with the free tools, so this is **unverified** [A].
- The phrase names the statutory test and is used descriptively by dozens of titles. Red Squirrel's "Life in the UK Test: …" series has run for 10+ editions [V]. Use as a descriptive term appears tolerated.
- Real restrictions: (1) do **not** use "Official", "Home Office", "approved", GOV.UK styling or the Royal Arms. The OGL non-endorsement clause and Amazon's misleading-metadata rules both apply. Several KDP titles skate close with "Official Style". (2) The booking provider warns: "There are many unofficial, unapproved learning materials… You are strongly advised to prepare using only the official learning materials… to avoid incorrect or misleading information" [V]. That will not stop sales, but it sets buyer expectations.
- **KDP public-domain/freely-available rule** [V kdp.amazon.com G200743940]: KDP "may also refuse public domain content that's already available through Amazon or other sites", and lists "Freely available Internet content" as *not* differentiating. Public-domain content is also not eligible for KDP Select or all royalty options. A book that is mostly the reproduced handbook therefore risks rejection or loss of KU. **A fully original Q&A book avoids this** [A].

## 4. Currency: edition and announced changes

- **Current handbook:** 3rd edition, published Jan 2013, for tests "taken on or after 25 March 2013" [V TSO shop, Wikipedia]. Earlier editions came in 2004 and 2007, so content changes have been rare (2004 → 2007 → 2013) [V Wikipedia].
- Amazon now lists the TSO book as "Official Guide… 2026", Edition "2023rd", dated 24 Mar 2023 [V]. A French review says it differs from 2016 with "new information related to the new King Charles" [V review text]. A reprint with the monarch reference updated is therefore *possible* but **unconfirmed** [A]. Third-party sites still say the book refers to the Queen (huntukvisasponsors, Sep 2026) [S].
- **Test format today:** 24 questions in 45 minutes, 75% (18) to pass, £50, booked ≥3 days ahead, 30+ centres [V gov.uk]. On the day there are also unscored warm-up questions [V PSI FAQ: "some non-scored questions"].
- **Announced changes:**
  - May 2025 White Paper: "We will also conduct a refresh of the Life in the UK test and how it operates" [V white paper PDF].
  - Nov 2025 earned-settlement consultation (*A Fairer Pathway to Settlement*): settlement still requires a pass, and the consultation lists "a formal test (such a revised Life in the UK Test)" as an option [V gov.uk].
  - 13 Jul 2026 parliamentary answer: the test "is being reviewed" to better reflect everyday life, with changes "announced in due course" with transitional arrangements [S immigrationbarrister.co.uk, 3 Aug 2026; Legal Centre FB post similar].
  - As of Sep–Oct 2026 nothing has changed and no new syllabus has been published [S withrowan.co.uk 2 Sep 2026; adambernards.co.uk Oct 2026].
- **Procurement timeline (the strongest signal):**
  - Home Office preliminary market engagement for the "Life in the UK Secure Digital Test and Digital Online Learning Materials Service": rounds on 6 Jan, 15 Jun and 11 Sep 2026; est. value £90.87m; contract period 24 Nov 2027–23 Nov 2032; "Digital-by-Default" [S enable.consulting citing FTS notices 000522-2026, 056070-2026, 086473/088893-2026].
  - PSI testing contract runs to 30 Sep 2027 [S stotles/tendersignal].
  - TSO "Test Preparation Materials" contract extended from 21 May 2027 to **30 Mar 2028** "whilst the replacement procurement is completed" [V ojeu FTS/095324-2026, published 8 Oct 2026].
  - [A] A new test and possibly new official learning materials (perhaps digital-first) are plausible from ~late 2027 to 2028. An existing book on the 2013 handbook would then be obsolete for new candidates, after a transition.
- Related policy that affects demand [S]: English requirement for settlement rises from B1 to B2 on 26 Mar 2027 (enacted). The 10-year baseline settlement period is still a proposal. [A] This may create a rush of ILR/citizenship applications before the rule changes, then a dip.

## 5. Feasibility for an AI-assisted writer with fact-checking

**What the best book looks like** (from praise and complaints):
- **Question volume:** competitors advertise 475+ (RS, older edition per Goodreads), 500 (Ixworth, GreatBrit), 1,300 (Matt Andy) and 12 full tests (Hugh Lewis). The official Practice Q&A has 17 quizzes [V US review]. Free sites offer 17 exams plus 45 tests. Target: **~800–1,000 original questions**, covering *every testable sentence*, mapped to the 5 chapters [A].
- **Explanations on every answer**, with the handbook chapter and section; the lack of them is a named complaint.
- **Full 24-question mock tests in print** (e.g. 20–25), plus chapter drills and a score tracker. No dependence on a website or app, since broken codes and sites are a recurring 2–3★ complaint.
- **"Outdated-fact trap" boxes** (Book says / Today / Answer in test). This is the clearest gap.
- **Memory aids:** timeline of dates, monarch table, "people & achievements" tables (sport, arts, science), four-nations comparison (parliaments, courts, saints' days), and a numbers table.
- **Kindle/mobile:** reflowable eBook with linked answer keys per quiz (answers right after each quiz, not at the back), KU enrollment, and flashcard-style sections. A paperback is needed too: buyers like "paper book in hand".
- **Scope honesty:** say plainly that real questions are unpublished and that the book trains the facts.

**Accuracy risk:**
- *Low-ish*: the testable universe is one ~180-page text, so every question can be traced to a sentence. A two-pass check (AI draft → human checks each item against the handbook → second-person spot audit) is tractable [A].
- *High-ish*:
  - (1) The book must teach the *2013 book's* "facts" even where they are wrong or outdated (population 62m, IPCC, Man Booker rules, Welsh Assembly). An AI or fact-checker using *current* knowledge will "correct" answers and make them wrong for the test.
  - (2) There are regional variants (a Scotland-based candidate gets questions on the Scottish Parliament) [V Wikipedia]. Questions must not assume England-only answers.
  - (3) Distractors in multiple choice must be unambiguously wrong *per the handbook*.
  - (4) Reddit shows ChatGPT-made mocks "make a few mistakes" and users warn "never ever use ChatGPT to construct mock questions" [V r/ukvisa]. Reviewers in this niche will punish a single wrong answer key.
- Realistic error rate: an unaudited AI draft could easily carry several percent wrong or ambiguous items [A, not measured]. After line-by-line handbook verification it should fall below 0.5% [A].
- *Shelf-life risk*: high, as described in §4. Plan a "2027 edition" refresh and a fast rewrite when a new handbook appears.

**Commercial fit [A]:**
- Price point is £6–£12 paperback and £3–£7 Kindle, KU-enabled; this matches the KDP entrants.
- Winning against TSO and Red Squirrel on the "handbook" query is unlikely. A realistic target is the "practice questions" sub-niche, where the top KDP title sits at BSR ~8k–10k.
- Differentiators are explanations, outdated-fact traps, all-in-print mocks, and no broken add-ons.
