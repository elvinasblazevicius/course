## 4. Safety and claims (for an author who is not a clinician)

### What the risk evidence says
- Therapy-practice sources (Calm, a Birmingham therapy practice, Annie Wright) warn that solo shadow work can bring unresolved trauma to the surface. People can become flooded or dissociated when the work moves "faster than the nervous system can integrate." They advise against deep solo work for people with complex trauma, dissociation or acute crisis [V via search summaries; these are practice blogs, not formal clinical guidance].
- Research on expressive writing is mixed and the effects are small. A search snippet cites Frattaroli's 2006 meta-analysis at an effect size of about .075 [V snippet]. The book should not claim it "heals trauma."

### What the book must avoid
1. **Medical or therapeutic claims.** No "heal your trauma," "therapy in a book," "therapist approved" (unless a named, credentialed person really reviewed it), "cure," or "PTSD recovery." Say "reflection" and "self-understanding," not "treatment."
2. **Prompts that ask readers to relive specific trauma in detail**: abuse scenes, sexual violence, "describe your worst memory." Also no prompts on suicidal ideation or self-harm history. Stay with patterns, reactions and present-day triggers rather than reconstructing past events in detail.
3. **Pushing confrontation**: no prompts like "write the letter, then send it" or "confront your parent." Unsent letters only, and say so.
4. **Blaming everything on childhood.** Business Insider flagged this. Offer other explanations too (temperament, ADHD, circumstances, chance).
5. **Fake or misattributed Jung.** Check every quote against a source; the Campbell line is a known trap. Never use a Jung photo of uncertain origin. Present the material as "Jung-inspired" and say plainly where modern pop shadow work differs from analytical psychology. That honesty is itself a selling point against "not real Jung" complaints.
6. **Promising integration or transcendence.** A Jungian reviewer mocked that language. Use "befriending," "noticing," "owning," "a lifelong practice."
7. **Spiritual, occult or manifestation claims**, if the book is meant to be faith-neutral or faith-friendly.

### Grounding, stop signals and help resources (done honestly)
- **Before using the book, a "Who this is not for right now" page:** people in active crisis, anyone with recent trauma, dissociation, flashbacks, a PTSD/CPTSD diagnosis without a therapist, active self-harm, or psychosis history. Phrase it as "please use this alongside support," not "you are forbidden."
- **A 0–10 check-in before and after every session.** Plain rule: if you are at 6 or higher, close the book and do a grounding exercise; come back another day.
- **A traffic-light key on prompts**: green (light), amber (go slowly), red (best with a supporter or therapist). Readers can skip any prompt, and the book should say so.
- **Short sessions with a closing ritual**: about 15–20 minutes (a practice-blog suggestion [V]), then a closing step every time (a feet-on-the-floor, 5-4-3-2-1 senses check, or a kettle-and-window moment).
- **A repeated "Stop if" box**: racing heart that won't settle, feeling unreal or numb, memories you didn't choose flooding in, urges to hurt yourself.
- **A help page at the front and back.** US: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988). UK/ROI: Samaritans 116 123. UK: Shout (text 85258). Elsewhere: findahelpline.com. Emergency: 911/999/112. [A: standard numbers from general knowledge; check each one at publication.] Also explain how to find a trauma-informed therapist, and that some therapists will work through a workbook with clients.
- **Honest author note**: "I'm not a therapist. This is a companion for reflection, written with care, checked against [sources]. It is not treatment." This also answers the "influencer credentials" criticism.
- **Format**: if prompts are not in the Kindle file, do not sell the Kindle edition as a "workbook." Either include the prompts as text, or label it clearly and offer printable PDF pages. Use a lie-flat-friendly interior with generous gutters.

## 5. Gaps: where a warm, plain-spoken, gently humorous guide could win

The number-one complaint across all the top books is shallow, repetitive questions with no guidance, and theory that is either front-loaded or missing. Fixing that does not require a new topic. It requires short teaching before each prompt, a worked example of an answer, and a "what this might mean" note after. The Renner books, which carry the most teaching content, are already the 2025 independent winners. That suggests buyers are moving toward more teaching [V for the BSRs, A for the reason].

**Recommended positioning [A]:** *"Shadow work for women in the second half of life — a gentle, honest guide with real teaching between the prompts."* For example: 12 weeks with 5 short sessions a week, about 25k words of teaching and stories, and 60–90 prompts. Each prompt comes with a worked example and a "what you might notice" note. Grounding and stop signals are built into every session. Jung is explained plainly, with myths and misquotes corrected and the humour kept gentle. Paperback interior that lies flat. A Kindle edition labelled "guide" with prompts included as text.

## Scores (10 = best for us)
- **Demand: 7/10.** An evergreen anchor (Shaheen at about BSR 4.6k) and a new independent title at BSR 3k show that buyers keep coming. Against that, keyword interest is falling (Exploding Topics 14.8K → 6.6K, −33%) and the 2023 peak is over.
- **Competition: 3/10 (10 = easy).** Over 2,000 US results, template floods, 46 of the 48 newest titles at zero ratings, and the top spots locked by Shaheen and Renner. The under-served sub-niches are open but have little proven demand.
- **Safety and claims risk: 5/10 (10 = low risk without a human expert).** The content is trauma-adjacent, but the risk can be managed with careful prompts, no therapy claims, grounding and help pages. The residual risk is a reader in crisis.
- **Fit for an emotional, human-voiced 20–30k-word book: 8/10.** The leading complaints (no guidance, repetitive, shallow, cold) are exactly what a warm teaching voice fixes. A journal-buying audience still expects writing space, though, so plan for about 160–220 pages.

**Best angle:** a gentle, honest shadow-work guide-journal for women in midlife and after 50, with a short teaching passage and a worked example before every prompt.

**Biggest risk:** a crowded, declining keyword. A well-made book in an unproven sub-niche (over-50) may get no search traffic, while the broad keywords are owned by Shaheen, the two Renner titles and the ad spenders.
