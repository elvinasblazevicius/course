## The book (draft)
A **large-print cozy (non-gory) village murder mystery told through word searches**, for adults 55+ and cozy-mystery readers.
- **Story:** one continuous case. Each puzzle opens with a short scene (a witness statement, a note, an alibi) and hides a word list of names, places and clues.
- **Mechanism:** each solved grid gives one real story clue plus one leftover hidden letter. Collected, the letters spell the answer to the final reveal: who did it, how and why. The puzzles feed the plot, which is the gap readers name in the comps.
- **Verification by code:** every word placed and findable exactly once; the leftover-letter message is exact; every clue needed for the solution is present, with no contradictions (a logic checker over the case facts).
- **Large print:** 8.5x11, grids about 15x15 with letters of 16pt or more, high-contrast black, solutions with the hidden words outlined.
- **Series:** volume 1 non-dated "Winter" (sells past Christmas), then seasonal and setting volumes. It cross-sells with KDP-000071 *Large Print Word Search for Adults and Seniors*.
- **Naming:** own coined series name; no "Murd-", "-doku", Clue/Cluedo, Hunt A Killer, or "The Killer Isn't [Name]" pattern.

- **Gap:** plain large-print murder word searches do poorly (#283k, best has 4 ratings). Readers complain the cozy word searches don't feed the plot, and that giant name-list books strain the eyes.
- **Review matrix:** 58 Goodreads 1–3★ reviews, covering:
  - ambiguous or "missing information" clues
  - typos
  - solutions that don't explain why
  - too easy or too hard
  - small print
  - stories that are too thin
  - one review that calls a book "AI-generated"

## Main risks
1. Flood of copies since June 2026; comparable titles at 3.0–3.6★.
2. One ambiguous clue brings "unsolvable" 1★ reviews while the book has few ratings.
3. The story text is the AI-hunt exposure. It must be written to the skill's voice standard, short and specific.
4. Trend durability (wave began with Murdle in 2023).
5. A Christmas launch (live by about Nov 15) is possible but needs Amazon Ads. Organic top rank is unlikely this season.

**Gate: NOT PASSED.**

**Root causes:**
1. **The mechanism is not new.** About 12 cozy story word-search series already do "puzzles feed the plot":
   - Murder for Breakfast/Oakford Springs: 4.8★, 192 ratings, #5,298.
   - Cranberry Creek (Adams Media): #1,837, with a second series due Nov 2026.
   - Kill Grid: #700.
2. **Large print is unproven and counter-evidenced.** Elmbrook, an 8.5x11 large-print near-copy of this book, is at #1.09M with 2 ratings; Cozy Case Files LP is at #283k. All the winners are 6x9 novel-like books.
3. **Winners compete on story quality,** which is prose. That is the AI-hunt exposure Elvin wants to avoid.
4. **A fair-play mystery is hard to prove by code.** In a toy case, the checker found three possible culprits under the reader's open-world reading.

**Settled facts from the panel:**
- Print only: KDP's Kindle quality guide lists puzzle books as unsuited to Kindle.
- Disclose AI-drafted story text on KDP.
- The new KDP cap is 2 new titles per format per week (per a secondary source quoting KDP).
- Christie, Marple, Poirot and Murder, She Wrote are trademarks; avoid them.
- Brand safety next to the dementia and loss titles: death off the page, no elderly or care-home victims, never marketed as a dementia activity book.
