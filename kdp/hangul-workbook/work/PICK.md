# N1 pick brief: Korean Hangul course workbook (2026-10-15)

## Concept (from deep_hangul.md)
"Read Hangul in 14 Days" is a code-built course workbook for teens and adults (K-pop/K-drama fans, travellers, heritage learners).

**Contents**
- The 24 basic letters and the compound letters, taught in a fixed order.
- Syllable-block builder exercises.
- Reading drills that use only words made from letters already taught.
- Engine-checked sound-change drills.
- Romanization that fades out by week 2.
- A native-handwriting comparison page.
- Review tests and full answer keys.
- Mouth-shape diagrams and minimal-pair drills in place of audio.

**Format:** 8.5x11, B&W, about 200 pp, $12.99–14.99. Print only, or a fixed-layout Kindle edition if KDP allows it.

**Series:** a Korean sound-change book, then Japanese kana, then Arabic.

## Why it fits Elvin's preferences (kdp/PREFERENCES.md)
- **AI-hunt immunity:** content is data, drills and answer keys, with almost no prose.
- **High content:** no blank padding.
- **No human checker:** most of it is verified by code.
  - Syllables are composed from Unicode jamo.
  - Romanization uses 2–3 engines (koroman scored 24/25 on the official rule examples); only words where they agree are kept.
  - Stroke order follows the conventional school order, cross-checked across sources. The National Institute of Korean Language says stroke count is not fixed by the spelling rules.
  - Word glosses are checked against the institute's dictionary and Wiktionary data (CC BY-SA, so check only, don't copy).

## Evidence
- **Leader:** *The One and Only Korean Workbook* (Self-taught KOR, 2025), BSR 8,916, 4.7★ from 223 ratings. It has a 30-page intro, 100+ pages of graded exercises, YouTube videos and a Quizlet deck with audio. Three Korean sister titles (best #16,967); it is ad-backed.
- **Others:** about 6 Hangul titles under BSR 90k; four older titles have 1,100–2,029 ratings. Most 2025–26 newcomers outside that brand sit at #600k–3.4M.
- **Reviews:**
  - Main complaint: no audio.
  - Also: English typos, odd translations, too much romanization, "rest all is blank notebook", thin instructions.
  - No complaints found about stroke order or "AI".
- **Trend:** Korean is #6 on Duolingo (2025). US Korean learners are up 22% (KPop Demon Hunters, Feb 2026). Duolingo added a Hangul tab in Jul 2026.
