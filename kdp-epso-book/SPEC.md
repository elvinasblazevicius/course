# Book Specification — Reasoning Tests Workbook for EPSO Exams (rev. 3, G1 PASS 9/10)

## Positioning
- **Title:** *Reasoning Tests Workbook for EPSO Exams*
- **Subtitle:** *400+ Verbal, Numerical and Abstract Reasoning Practice Questions, Situational Judgement and Assistant-Level Skills Tests, 3 Full Mock Exams and Fully Worked Solutions*
- **Imprint / series line:** "Concours Prep" (neutral independent imprint; "EU Careers" avoided because it is EPSO’s own recruitment brand — G4 legal finding)
- **Audience:** Candidates for EU institution selection procedures organised by EPSO (AD and AST generalist and specialist competitions, AST-SC and CAST), plus anyone preparing for European-style psychometric reasoning tests.
- **Coverage statement (Chapter 1, the back cover and the description):**
  - The verbal, numerical and abstract reasoning tests are the core reasoning tests used across generalist AD and AST competitions.
  - Situational judgement, accuracy & precision and prioritising & organising tests have appeared mainly in assistant-level (AST-SC), CAST and some specialist procedures.
  - This book does **not** cover EU-knowledge, digital-skills or language tests.
  - EPSO runs the reasoning tests in the candidate's chosen language; this book is in English.
  - Readers must always check the Notice of Competition for their procedure.
- **Evergreen rule:** The book contains no competition dates, no claims about exact current question counts, timings or pass marks, and no EU-institutional facts that go out of date. All passages are self-contained.
- **Originality statement:** Every question is original and written for this book. None is reproduced from EPSO or from any other publisher.

## Structure
**Front matter:** title page; copyright, disclaimer and originality page; contents; how to use this book; 4-week and 8-week study plans.

**Part I — Understanding the tests (~25 pp)**
1. EU selection tests: which test types apply to which procedures, the typical computer-based multiple-choice format, no negative marking in typical formats, language note, and why to check the Notice of Competition
2. Verbal reasoning: the "only what the text says" principle and trap types
3. Numerical reasoning toolkit: percentages, percentage points, percentage change, ratios, averages, currency conversion, compound growth, reading charts, estimation
4. Abstract reasoning: a rule-hunting checklist
5. Situational judgement: typical competency themes and how to rate actions
6. Accuracy & precision; prioritising & organising
7. Test-day strategy: pacing, guessing, flagging, calculators, test-centre versus remote sittings

**Part II — Practice sets (graded from foundation to advanced)**
| Test | Items | Options |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal reasoning | 65 | 4 (A–D) |
| Numerical reasoning | 75 | 4 (A–D), as in typical formats |
| Abstract reasoning | 75 | 5 (A–E) |
| Situational judgement | 24 | 4 actions, choose MOST and LEAST |
| Accuracy & precision | 30 | 4 |
| Prioritising & organising | 24 | 4 |

**Part III — Three timed mock exams.** Each has 20 verbal + 10 numerical + 10 abstract questions (40 in total) = 120 items, with a suggested time limit, an answer grid and score bands.

**Total: 413 questions.**

**Part IV — Answers and full worked solutions for EVERY item**
- Verbal: why the key is correct, and the trap type behind each distractor.
- Numerical: step-by-step calculation, plus the error that produces each distractor.
- Abstract: the rule(s) stated explicitly, and why each other option breaks a rule.
- Situational judgement: the effectiveness of every option, the full ranking and the competency.
- Accuracy & precision: the exact discrepancy located.
- Prioritising & organising: the constraint-by-constraint reasoning.

**Back matter:** score tracker, error log and answer sheets.

## Correctness assurance
- Code-generated items (numerical, abstract, accuracy, prioritising): answers are computed by the program. The code asserts exactly one valid option, distinct options and a balanced answer-letter distribution with no runs of 3 or more identical keys.
- Abstract reasoning additionally gets a **competing-rule test**: each distractor is checked against every alternative rule in the generator's rule library, and an item is rejected if any distractor fits a plausible rule for the sequence.
- Prioritising items are brute-force solved, and the code asserts a unique feasible answer.
- **Independent blind solving:** a reviewer agent solves samples of every test type without seeing the key, and every mismatch is investigated and fixed.
- Verbal and situational items are hand-authored to a template and reviewed item by item by an independent reviewer.
